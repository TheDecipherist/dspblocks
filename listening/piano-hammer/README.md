# Milestone 2: a felt hammer, not a trigger

The author, on milestone 1: *"honestly that sounds like an acoustic guitar mixed with a
harpsichord."* Correct, and precise - a harpsichord IS a plucked stiff string,
which is exactly what milestone 1 was. This is the set that answers it.

**1 - pluck against strike.** Same string, same everything. The pluck releases
it from a corner so every partial fires at once; the strike presses felt against
it for two milliseconds, which cannot reach the top of the series at all.
Measured attack centroid: pluck 7694 Hz, strike 357 Hz. **Does the strike sound
struck rather than plucked?**

**2 - velocity, three blows.** The point is NOT that the loud one is louder.
Match them by ear on your monitor and the hard one should STILL be brighter,
because the felt stiffens under load, contact shortens from 4.81 ms to 2.31 ms,
and a shorter contact reaches higher partials. That is velocity changing the
sound rather than the volume. **Does hard sound like a different sound, or the
same sound turned up?**

**3 - soft felt against hard felt, same velocity.** A voicer's needle in a
file. Attack centroid 222 Hz against 444 Hz.

**4 - two registers.** A1 with B = 1e-3 and A4 with B = 5e-5, both struck. The
bass should be the metallic, stretched one.

## What is still missing, so you know what you are judging

**One string per note.** A real piano has 1 in the low bass, 2 through the
tenor, and 3 for most of the keyboard, tuned a couple of cents apart on purpose.
That unison is where the shimmer comes from AND where the two-stage decay comes
from: in phase they dump energy into the bridge fast, then they drift out of
phase, stop driving the bridge, and ring on quietly for a long time. A single
string can only give one exponential, so these still decay like a guitar. That
is milestone 3 and it is the biggest remaining gap.

**No soundboard and no body at all.** Not a wrong body - none. Milestone 4.

Levels are unnormalised on purpose.
